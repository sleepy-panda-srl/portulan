Portulan is an operating framework for agentic engineering: the tailored context, standards,
gates, and institutional memory a team needs so any coding agent works *their* way. **The engine
is the chartmaking method; your workspace is your portulan.** The method is free. What Sleepy
Panda sells is the labor of applying it and keeping it true.

## Free — the method

Everything in this repository, under Apache-2.0, forever: the engine (`core/`), the Workspace
Definition (`spec/`), all eight CLI subcommands (`init` · `doctor` · `compile` · `vendor` ·
`index` · `upgrade` · `new` · `feedback`), the Claude Code plugin, the universal packs, and a
complete demo workspace to read. Run it in production. Nothing in the free layer phones home,
meters seats, or expires — the product is its files, and the files are yours once you have them.

What the free layer honestly is: a framework you apply yourself. `init` drafts a workspace and
composes the checkpoint ritual by default; `doctor` grades it; `compile` turns your gate policy
into host enforcement; the drafting is the easy afternoon. The curation — what your gates should
be, what green means in your CI, which rules your incidents have earned — is the work, and it is
yours whether or not you hire us for it.

## Chartmaking — the onboarding engagement

**$10,000, one-time, fixed scope.** We author your portulan with you: the `init` interview and
codebase scan, then human curation with your team — identity, principles, and definition-of-done
written in your terms; a gate map argued tier by tier; verify recipes bound to your real CI and
demonstrated red→green, not asserted; the supervised-evolution cadence installed (checkpoints,
handoff records, memory with provenance); roughly thirty days of async iteration after the
workshop. The deliverable is your workspace, in your repositories or your own private feed, **your
property outright** — not licensed, not hosted by us, not dependent on us. If we disappear the day
after delivery, everything keeps working.

## Chart-keeping — the curation retainer

**$1,500–3,000 per month, by cadence (monthly or weekly pass).** The framework defines a
librarian and a supervisor; the retainer staffs those roles across your boundary until your own
team does. Each pass: mine your incidents and reviews into drafted rules with provenance,
consolidate memory against its budgets, keep your compiled gates current as hosts and the
Workspace Definition move (`upgrade` is the mechanism; we run it and hand you the diff), and
answer async questions. **We draft, measure, and nag; your humans approve** — supervised
evolution is the constitution's rule and it does not bend for us. The retainer's stated goal is
making itself unnecessary: design for deletion applies to the vendor too.

Stopping the retainer stops the passes and nothing else. There is no feed to lose access to, no
license to lapse — your workspace is yours, and the last pass's output stays exactly as delivered.

## What is deliberately not for sale yet

**A premium pack catalog.** The open-core design reserves room for paid packs delivered through
private feeds, and the mechanics are built and demonstrated. The catalog is not: this repository
ships two public packs today, and a pack is sold the day its upkeep is funded and not before — a
subscription to content nobody is maintaining would be exactly the kind of unfunded promise this
framework exists to prevent. If retainer work keeps surfacing the same generic pattern, it will be
re-expressed into a pack (never absorbing any customer's specifics — one-way flow), and priced
then.

**A hosted anything.** No SaaS, no dashboard, no telemetry. The one doctrine-permitted exception —
an approval relay for async gated actions — will ship self-hostable first when its milestone
arrives; it does not exist today and nothing here depends on it.

## Buying

Both paid products are fixed-price and bookable without a call. Until the milestone-10 checkout
ships, founding engagements are arranged by email — and "founding" is meant literally: the first
adopter cycle outside this repository has not run yet, the framework versions its spec at `0.x`,
and early customers get the prices above held for their first year in exchange for being the
proof. That trade is stated rather than hidden because the alternative — a pricing page that
implies a queue of references — would fail this repository's own definition of done.
