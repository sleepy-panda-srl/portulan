## Amendment — 2026-07-28 (an expansion)

**Criterion amended with Marius, 2026-07-28, this row only — an expansion, not a narrowing, checked in the way the M1–M4 amendments established.** Four clauses are added and none removed: **(a) adversarial fixtures per compiled gate** — golden tasks reach the **gates** as well as the skills, so a matcher ships with the attack cases that prove its coverage instead of prose describing it; **(b) mutation testing over both matchers, and grammar-aware fuzzing over the shell segmenter**; **(c) review-loop metering in the telemetry clause** — rounds per pull request, pushes per round, empty-round rate; **(d) scheduled forced-red drills** — every rail forced red on a calendar and required to fire. **Checked as an expansion:** all four add deliverables; golden tasks per core skill, the A/B baseline, the OTel opt-in config and the eval-gated rule change each stand exactly as written, and (a) widens the golden-task clause's **subject** without narrowing the subject it already had. **Each clause is here because these two days did it by hand, and the record says so.** (a) and (b): [#60](https://github.com/sleepy-panda-srl/portulan/pull/60)'s constitution write-gate was called *done*, after which a supervisor's attack pass found **five holes the change's own published hole list had missed** — that list had four entries and was itself wrong, which is why the lesson is that a hole list is a claim like any other — and fourteen review rounds then found **three more live bypasses the attack pass itself had not tried**. Eight in all, every one found after the gate was called done and every one closed before it merged. Seven of the eight are grammar edge cases a fuzzer enumerates cheaply — a newline, a CRLF continuation, an escaped quote, `{`/`then`/`do` leaders, the wrapper not first on the line, and two path spellings; the eighth, deleting the container out from under the file, is an equivalence oracle's catch rather than a fuzzer's, and mutation testing is the half of (b) that reaches it. (c): the *110 rounds over 30 pull requests* figure that bounds the review loop was measured **by hand**, and [`../.portulan/memory/a-review-loop-needs-a-bound.md`](../../.portulan/memory/a-review-loop-needs-a-bound.md) says of itself that **nothing checks it** — a mandate with no checker, which [`../.portulan/memory/a-mandate-nothing-checks-is-already-broken.md`](../../.portulan/memory/a-mandate-nothing-checks-is-already-broken.md) holds to be another way of spelling broken; the telemetry clause is where that checker's home is, and naming the home is what this amendment does rather than claiming the checker exists. (d) generalises proposal [`0007`](../../.portulan/proposals/0007-every-watcher-ships-with-its-observation-procedure.md), *a watcher earns its place by being watched*, along both of its axes: from **one demonstration at adoption** — the proposal's rule is that the procedure is *"run once and its result recorded"* — to a calendar, and from **watchers** to every rail. Without it a rail that has quietly stopped firing is found by the incident rather than by the drill. **Why this row and not sooner:** all four are evals and telemetry, and built before the harness that keeps them honest they would be rails with nothing measuring them — the failure this row exists to prevent. Provenance: the two-day review of 2026-07-27/28, R4 and R11, delegated as the R4+R11 item of the **Session B commission of 2026-07-28**.


## Amendment — 2026-08-24 (an expansion)

**Criterion amended with Marius, 2026-08-24, this row only — an expansion, not a narrowing, checked in the way the M1–M7 amendments established.** One clause is added and none removed: **a release carries an eval result.**

**Why it is an amendment rather than a clarification.** The obligation is not new — [`../plan.md`](../plan.md)'s Protocol → Versioning has said *"from milestone 8, releases carry an eval result"* since the plan was locked on 24 July 2026, and [`../../CHANGELOG.md`](../../CHANGELOG.md)'s own header repeats it. What was new on 2026-08-24 is that the sentence **binds this row's close**. Until then it belonged to no row: milestone 8's criterion listed eight deliverables and that was not among them, so a milestone-close reviewer re-deriving the criterion clause by clause would have re-derived eight and closed the row with a plan-level milestone-8 obligation unbuilt. **A mandate stated in the Protocol and owned by no row is the shape [`../../.portulan/memory/a-mandate-nothing-checks-is-already-broken.md`](../../.portulan/memory/a-mandate-nothing-checks-is-already-broken.md) names**, one altitude up: not a rule with no checker, but a rule with no *owner*.

**How it surfaced.** Milestone 8 session 0 named it in [`../../evals/README.md`](../../evals/README.md)'s *What is NOT built yet* list — deliberately, as an item belonging to a later session **or to the maintainer's release procedure**, since the session could not tell which and would not decide for him. The session-open checkpoint had raised the same gap as adjustment 8 and the session's answer had been to name it rather than to assign it. He assigned it: *"M8 can't be closed without addressing this item."*

**Checked as an expansion**, the way the amendments to date established: it adds a deliverable and removes, narrows or conditions nothing. The eight clauses of the 2026-07-28 amendment and the four original subjects stand exactly as written.

**What it does NOT settle**, stated so the close cannot be held to more than was ruled: *what* an eval result attached to a release consists of, *where* it is carried (the changelog entry, the release body, a file in the payload), and whether a release with no eval result is refused by a rail or by a person. Those are this row's remaining work, not this amendment's content.

## Session note — 2026-08-25, session 1: the budget, ruled

**The number arrived, and it is not a number.** The maintainer's ruling of 2026-08-25: the Sessions cell reads **`1 per clause`**.

**Why that is the right shape rather than a figure.** A fixed count would have to be re-derived every time the row is amended, and this row has been amended twice — the 2026-07-28 expansion added four clauses and the 2026-08-24 expansion added a ninth. A cell reading *9* would have been wrong on 2026-07-27 and wrong again the day before it was written; a cell reading *1 per clause* was correct on all three days and stays correct through the next amendment. It is the same repair this milestone has now applied to recipe counts, corpus sizes and operator totals — **state the rule, not the tally** — applied to the plan's own budget column.

**What it binds.** One session per clause, so the row's budget now moves with its criterion instead of against it. Sessions 0 and 1 each delivered exactly one clause, which is the cadence this ratifies rather than introduces. Seven clauses remain and therefore seven sessions, and an amendment adding a clause adds a session without anybody editing this cell.

**What it does not license.** It is a budget, not a floor: a session that lands its clause is done, and a clause that turns out to need two sessions is a conversation with the maintainer rather than a licence the cell grants. The ruling of 2026-08-24 stands unchanged above it — *no clause is trimmed to fit the cell*. Recorded here rather than in the row, since the row carries the criterion and this is a note about how long meeting it takes.

## Session note — 2026-08-25, session 2: clause (d), and what "every rail" turned out to mean

**One clause, as the budget now reads.** Session 2 took **(d)**, scheduled forced-red drills, and six
clauses remain. It was the clause the repository had already named as next, in two places rather than
one: [`../../evals/README.md`](../../evals/README.md)'s arrival sentence for (d) said the drills of
sessions 0 and 1 were *"run by hand, in a session … which is precisely the state (d) exists to replace"*,
and [`../../.portulan/verify/README.md`](../../.portulan/verify/README.md)'s register subsection called
itself *"the register the calendar writes into"* while no calendar existed. A written-down dependency
discharged, the same shape as (a)→(b).

**The reach was the decision, and it is settled by what a drill can force rather than by what sounds
complete.** The 2026-07-28 amendment says (d) generalises
[`0007`](../../.portulan/proposals/0007-every-watcher-ships-with-its-observation-procedure.md) along both
axes — one demonstration to a calendar, and watchers to **every rail**. What that resolves to here is
every recipe the workspace yields, plus the Stop-gate and the PreToolUse gate runner: twenty-one rails,
all of them forced red and every one fired. The rails a local process cannot force are **printed on every
run with their reasons** — the platform floor, the host's permission layer, the CI seam, the pre-commit
seam scan, `claude plugin validate --strict`, the platform watchers, and the librarian's pass. Two of
those seven were added at the session-open checkpoint, which found the exclusion list short.

**What the clause does not settle, stated so a close cannot be held to more than was built.** A drill
establishes that a rail fires on **one** known-bad input, never that it catches everything — the same
presence-versus-adequacy boundary (a) already draws. And a *missing* scheduled run stays undetectable:
GitHub delays schedules under load and disables them after 60 days of inactivity, so no sweep and no
failure look alike. That is `0007`'s silence problem one altitude up, tracked as
[#344](https://github.com/sleepy-panda-srl/portulan/issues/344) rather than built, because closing it
needs a second mechanism — a committed dated register with a staleness rail over it — and folding that in
would have been a second clause's work inside a one-clause budget.
