## Amendment — 2026-07-30 (an expansion) — RATIFIED 2026-07-31 and applied, with the subcommand left unassigned

**Applied to the row**, with **one clause taken the other way** — see *One clause routed to the
maintainer* below, which is where that choice was framed. Recorded here rather than only in a
pull-request body because an amendment argument belongs beside the row it argues about, and a
criterion change that lives only in a merged conversation is a rule with no carrier.

**What changed between the draft and the row.** The draft assigned the switch to `vendor`; the
maintainer ratified the amendment and **declined that assignment**, taking the second of the two
options the draft offered him. So the row says *"the CLI performs the switch in both directions"* and
names no subcommand, with `vendor` recorded as the candidate and the verb left to milestone 7's
design. `init` and `upgrade` keep their assignments, which were never in question. The draft below is
left **exactly as it was written** — a proposal records what was argued when it was argued, and
editing it to match the ruling would destroy the record that makes the ruling legible; the row carries
the ratified form.

**Proposed row text, verbatim as drafted — the row differs in the `vendor` clause only:**

> **Amended 2026-07-30 (an expansion; argument in [`milestones/m07.md`](m07.md)).** Added,
> with nothing removed: the CLI carries the **residence choice and the switch** that proposal
> [`0017`](../../.portulan/proposals/0017-one-repository-one-governing-workspace.md) rules on. `init` asks
> where this repository's workspace resides — in the repository, or in a workspace that names it — and
> writes a full workspace or a pointer accordingly. `vendor` performs the **switch** in both directions,
> feed-side ↔ in-repo, under the contract the proposal sets: the workspace is materialised in the new
> residence, a pointer or nothing is left in the old, and **`doctor` is green at both ends before the
> old residence is retired**. `upgrade` migrates a workspace in **either** residence. And a **fourth
> demonstration** joins the row's three: **parity** — the same workspace exercised in both residences
> with no functionality difference, and the switch run in both directions.

_**The two relative links above are re-based one directory deeper so they resolve from this file**, the
same convention this milestone's header describes for the material moved here in the post-M5
reconciliation. Applied to the row in [`../plan.md`](../plan.md) they become
`(../.portulan/proposals/0017-one-repository-one-governing-workspace.md)` and `(milestones/m07.md)`.
Every target is unchanged; only the number of `../` moves._

**Checked as an expansion, in the way the M1–M4 amendments established.** It adds deliverables to
`init`, `vendor` and `upgrade` — all three already named in the row — and a fourth demonstration beside
the row's existing three. Every existing clause stands unchanged; nothing is removed, narrowed or made
conditional.

**Why it lands here rather than in row 6.** Row 6 ships the feed and the portfolio workspace; the
residence *choice* is a thing the CLI does to a customer's repository, and this is the CLI's row. The
ruling itself is recorded and railed at row 6's edge — the pointer kind, and `doctor`'s refusals — so
what remains for row 7 is the mechanics: asking, switching, migrating, and demonstrating parity.

**Why parity needs a demonstration rather than an assertion.** The proposal's argument is that features
key to workspace *slots* and never to residence, which is checkable by reading the tools — but "no
functionality difference" is a claim about behaviour, and this project's own bar is that behaviour is
exercised rather than asserted (the verification hierarchy: compiles < tests pass < behaviour
exercised). One workspace, both residences, the same operations, and the switch run in **both**
directions — because a one-way switch demonstrated once is a migration, not a choice.

**One clause routed to the maintainer rather than ratified quietly: `vendor` as the switch's carrier.**
[`../vision.md`](../vision.md) glosses `vendor` as *"self-contained AGENTS.md + .portulan/ for any
host"*. Feed-side → in-repo is exactly that shape: materialising a self-contained workspace into a
repository is what the gloss describes. The **reverse** direction — in-repo → feed-side, producing a
workspace that ships as a plugin through a private feed — sits outside the gloss, and calling it
`vendor` stretches a word that currently means something narrower. There is no contradiction, and the
row becomes law once ratified, so the stretch is named rather than absorbed. Two ways to take it, and
the choice is the maintainer's:

- **Keep `vendor` for both directions**, on the reading that the operation is one operation —
  materialise this workspace *there* — and the direction is an argument to it rather than a different
  verb. The gloss then widens by one clause, which is a vision edit and therefore his.
- **Leave the subcommand unassigned in the row** — "the CLI performs the switch in both directions" —
  and let milestone 7's design settle which verb carries it, with `vendor` the candidate.

The text above takes the first, because a row that names no subcommand is a row that has deferred the
part a reader most wants; but the second is a one-line change to the proposed text and costs nothing if
he prefers it.

**Ruled 2026-07-31: the second.** Put to him at milestone 7's session-open alongside the amendment
itself, he ratified the amendment and left the subcommand unassigned. The row therefore reads *"the
CLI performs the switch in both directions"*, and states in its own text why the verb is deferred —
that `vision.md`'s gloss covers one direction and widening it is his edit — so a reader meets the
reason at the row rather than having to come here for it. `vendor` stays the candidate. **What this
costs, named rather than discovered later:** milestone 7's design owes a verb, and the session that
picks one is choosing between widening the gloss (a `vision.md` change, his) and minting a seventh
subcommand (a `vision.md` change, also his). Either way the choice returns to him — the deferral moves
it, and does not remove it.

## Amendment — 2026-07-30 (an expansion) — the supervised cycle, instantiated

**Applied to the row** on the day it was drafted — the residence amendment above was drafted the same
day and was **ratified and applied on 2026-07-31**, so both now stand in row 7. _(This paragraph said
the residence amendment was one row 7 "does not yet carry", which was true when written and stopped
being true when that amendment was ratified; corrected in the same change that ratified it.)_ The two
argue different clauses and are independent: this one adds `init`'s cycle binding, pack-skill parity
and a parity clause; that one adds the residence choice and the switch. Neither narrows the other, and
row 7 reads correctly with either, both, or neither applied.

**Criterion amended with Marius, 2026-07-30, this row only — an expansion, not a narrowing, checked in the way the M1–M6 amendments established.** Added, with nothing removed: the three clauses that **instantiate** the supervised cycle in a customer's workspace. Every existing clause of row 7 stands exactly as written — `init`/`doctor`/`compile`/`vendor`/`index`/`upgrade`, the `new` and `feedback` subcommands, the three demonstrations, and the legibility score added on 2026-07-28 — and the three new clauses add deliverables to `init`, to the pack-composition path this row already owns through `vendor`, and to the demonstration list. **Checked as an expansion** on that basis.

**Why this row, and why now.** The same change that carries this amendment upgrades [`../../core/operating/evolution.md`](../../core/operating/evolution.md)'s customer-zero paragraph into a stated principle — *work is agent-drafted and fresh-context-graded at three moments, with hard exit criteria and a record every session* — recorded as [`0019`](../../.portulan/proposals/0019-the-development-cycle-is-doctrine-not-anecdote.md). The maintainer's question on the day it was decided was the right one: **does that happen today for an adopter?** The measured answer is *partially, and only for one half*.

- **Records: partially.** [`../../core/operating/loop.md`](../../core/operating/loop.md) mandates the dated handoff, [`../../core/templates/handoff.md`](../../core/templates/handoff.md) shapes it, the Workspace Definition has a `handoffs` slot, milestone 4 built the session-end gate, and milestone 5 built a librarian that reads the series generically. But the **rails are customer zero's** — wired in this repository's own compiled policy and its own `workspace.json`. A workspace drafted today receives the doctrine and none of the wiring.
- **Checkpoints: barely.** The ritual pack has existed since milestone 6 and **resolves from a feed**, which milestone 6 demonstrated. It is **opt-in**, nothing in the loop steps through it, and per [#134](https://github.com/sleepy-panda-works/portulan/issues/134) a feed-installed pack registers **`Skills (0)`** with the host — so everything that used those skills in milestone 6 did so **by a human naming a path**. That is not a cycle an adopter receives; it is files at a location.

So the doctrine `0019` states would, without this amendment, describe an experience only this repository has — which is the shape [`../../.portulan/dod.md`](../../.portulan/dod.md) condition 4 exists to refuse. This amendment is the sentence that names where the instantiation arrives, and it is deliberately a **row amendment rather than a core sentence**: core may not name a pack, a threshold, or a who, and every one of these three clauses is about binding exactly those.

**What the amendment deliberately does not do.** It does not make `init` author a curated layer — *"No auto-generated curated context. `init` drafts; humans accept"* is a binding non-goal and clause (a) says *drafted*, not *imposed*; the human still curates and may delete the binding outright, which is what makes it opt-**out**. It does not set a lane boundary: checkpoints bind full-lane work, and where that lane begins is the adopting workspace's threshold — the ritual pack refuses to set it and so does this. It does not generalise this repository's **Session-log** rails: the plan.md correspondence checks and the 10-line entry budget rail the build's own milestone map, a customer has no Session log, and the customer's records are the handoff series, memory, and the librarian that reads them. And it names **no specific pack** in core; the composition is the workspace's, and clause (a) says so.

**Provenance.** The maintainer's requirement of 2026-07-30, taken while deciding the two doctrine changes in [#137](https://github.com/sleepy-panda-works/portulan/pull/137): the shipped loop must include the build cycle's elements — pre-commit checkpoints and records-every-session — **instantiated in the customer's workspace, not hardcoded in core**. Drafted by the implementing session for his ratification; the session-open checkpoint ruled it into this pull request's diff rather than into a report, on the ground that every M1–M8 amendment landed as a merged diff and a body-only draft would owe a second act.


## Amendment — 2026-07-31 (an expansion) — a composed pack's verify recipes reach the workspace

**Applied to the row.** Ruled by the maintainer at this milestone's session-open, on a question this
repository had deliberately left open rather than answered by default.

**Criterion amended with Marius, 2026-07-31, this row only — an expansion, not a narrowing, checked in the way the M1–M6 amendments established.** Added, with nothing removed: a composed pack's verify recipes reach the adopting workspace's runnable set, additive-only, namespaced by pack, never the workspace's `verify.default`. Every existing clause of row 7 stands. **Checked as an expansion:** it adds a deliverable and a demonstration to a row that already ships pack composition through `vendor`, and removes, narrows and conditions nothing.

**What was actually open, and what was not.** The Pack Definition declares four contribution kinds and
two of them are consumed by nothing —
[#140](https://github.com/sleepy-panda-works/portulan/pull/140) measured the split kind by kind:
`gates` merged by `cli/compile.mjs`, `personas` resolved and opened by `cli/index.mjs`, `skills` and
`verify` by nothing. The session-open reconciliation found that **only one of those two was still
open**. Row 7's clause (b), applied on 2026-07-30, requires a composed pack's skills to be *invocable
through a host* with parity to a core skill — which cannot be reached without reading
`contributes.skills`, so that key's consumer was already committed and the schema note saying
otherwise had gone stale nine minutes after it landed. `verify` was the live question, and it is the
one the maintainer ruled.

**Why the additive-only contract is in the row rather than in the implementation.** Because it is the
part that can be got wrong silently. A recipe that composes without limits can shadow one the
workspace declares, or become the default the Stop-gate runs, and either would mean a workspace's
green stopped being a statement about the workspace. That is a criterion-level property — it decides
what *done* means for the adopting team — so it belongs where the criterion lives.

**The limit that is NOT claimed, said plainly because the first draft of this argument overclaimed
it.** The draft argued that namespacing and the no-`default` rule stop a pack author obtaining a
code-execution path into every adopting workspace's CI. That is wrong, and the session-open checkpoint
refused it: a recipe's `run` is arbitrary shell, CI runs the recipes the manifest carries, and a
workspace that lists a pack has already consented to running that pack's code — with or without these
clauses. What the clauses prevent is the narrower thing, which is also the thing worth preventing: a
pack **silently changing what this workspace's green means**. A security claim stronger than its
mechanism is the same defect this file's neighbours are full of, one layer up.

**Where it lands.** Session 2, beside the composition path `vendor` already owns — stated here because
an amendment that adds a deliverable without placing it is how a row grows an unscheduled obligation.

**Provenance.** Question 1 of milestone 7's session-open, put to the maintainer with both answers
drafted as row text — compose under a stated contract, or let declare-only stand permanently and
record it as settled rather than deferred. He ruled compose. The alternative is preserved in that
session's record rather than discarded, because the declare-only reading was defensible and a future
reader should be able to see that it was considered.
