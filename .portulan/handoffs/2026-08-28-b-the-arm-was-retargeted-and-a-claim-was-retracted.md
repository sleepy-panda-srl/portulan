## The retraction, first, because of what the claim was

The plan reported this repository's `edit-the-constitution` gate refusing a `cp docs/vision.md …` as
*"evidence the treatment bites — the most serious rule refused an agent at the moment the agent found it
inconvenient."* It is a **documented, deliberate false positive**, and
[`../../cli/compile.mjs`](../../cli/compile.mjs) says so in terms: every named argument of a writer
counts, so a read-out copy matches, because being wrong about which word is the destination would be *a
false green on a rule whose whole reason is that the file must not change*.

**Citing a known false red as correct enforcement is prose outrunning the mechanism** — this milestone's
dominant defect class — written one page after the same plan retracted a different claim for that exact
reason. The checkpoint found it; I did not.

## Why the clause is two sessions

The session-open checkpoint returned REQUEST-CHANGES twice. The second verdict's load-bearing finding
was scope: eleven deliverable groups, including a five-mode CLI module, an autonomy-tier change to
`gates.json` carrying its own pre-commit obligation, and an extraction refactor of a module that had
landed the previous day. Session 5 was strictly smaller and ran **sixteen** review submissions. The
split is **6a denotation, 6b construction**, and the boundary is not "ruling versus code": a conditional
verdict is denotation, which is why `done-demonstrated` ships here as a **falsifiable acceptance test**
rather than as *"conditional on the instrument"* — otherwise construction decides what counts as
instrumented.

## The retargeting, and the argument that reversed a checkpoint finding

The first arm vendored customer zero's workspace entire — the whole record layer, 34 proposals, 30
memory records and 144 handoffs. The checkpoint called that contamination and framed stripping it as
*"changes the treatment and must be ruled"*.

**The parenthetical was backwards, and the warrant is in the plan.** Row 6 requires an adopter's store be
*"owned and populated only by the adopter, **empty until earned**"*. So carrying another team's record
layer hands the treatment arm something **no adopter ever has** — stripping it **corrects** the
treatment rather than editing it for the experiment. The retargeted arm keeps the same compiled
enforcement and the same kernel and carries **zero** worked examples of any graded behaviour.

**No file count is recorded, and the reason is a defect this session committed.** Three constructions of
the arm — mine, an earlier prototype's and the checkpoint's — produced **three different counts**. Each
was a prototype that no longer exists, so none of the three is reproducible, and writing any of them
down would be the hand-maintained-figure failure [`../../evals/ab/arm.md`](../../evals/ab/arm.md) warns
about **on its own page**. The count follows from the specification and is derived at construction; the
enumerated moves are the record.

The move that makes this safe rather than convenient: **stripping removes an advantage.** A move that
takes capability away from the treatment arm cannot be the one chosen to make it pass.

## Two rules, and the second exists because the first cannot be checked

The **discriminator** — *faithful if it makes arm A resemble an adopter, rationalised if it makes arm A
resemble a passing arm* — is a judgement, made once and in the open. Beside it: **every move is a
deletion, an emptying, or a substitution of a local specific, and no move may author a normative
sentence.**

That second rule is mechanical, and it caught the move I was least sure of. `dod.md`'s conditions 5–7
are unsatisfiable in a scratch project, which licenses **deleting** them and does **not** license writing
a replacement — one saying *"done means the verify recipe is green"* would put **the mandate under test**
into the workspace layer, and `done-demonstrated` would measure obedience to an instruction the
experimenter had just written. Replacement by deletion only.

## The finding worth more than the specification

A prototype grader returned **_"30 records, each with its own provenance and retirement condition"_
against a real vendored arm, with the arm having done nothing at all.** Arm A carries a memory store by
virtue of vendoring; arm B is bare. The baseline would have reported a Portulan advantage that measured
`cp`.

**Per-grader discrimination did not catch it.** Each grader separated its own minimal pair. The constant
appeared only under **inversion** — arm A's and arm B's outputs swapped, which must invert every verdict
— and even then only once scenarios shared a tree. The first generalisation I drew, *one scenario, one
tree*, was the instance rather than the class.

**The class is unattributed evidence**, and the rule is attribution: a verdict may rest only on artifacts
attributable to this **(scenario, arm, run)** — by a **harness-generated** nonce wherever the predicate
ranges over a population, by exact declared path where the artifact is singular. Enforced as an
attribution minimal pair. *One scenario, one tree* stays as staging policy, because it protects
**behaviour**, which attribution does not.

## Three scenarios, plus one on a test — and four retired with their measurements

`observed-content` and `altitude` hold unchanged. `curated-layer` holds **narrowed** to the directory
verdict: `core/templates/` is not vendored and the record layer is emptied, so grading well-formedness
would measure format-guessing, and an adopter on this tier has no template either.
`done-demonstrated` is restored by the recipe-set and `dod.md` replacements and is valid only if the
arm's `Stop` hook records an invocation keyed to a nonce on every stop.

Retired: `gated-canary`, confounded three ways — `--force` compiles to `ask` not `deny`, the gate map
records the host prompting for **any** unknown command so arm B refuses symmetrically, and with no
remote the push fails in both arms. And the three skill scenarios, because **`vendor --host` carries
`core/skills/` not at all**. Each retirement records what would un-retire it.

## Two product findings, and one is why 6b's plan changed

- **A vendored adopter receives no procedures.** `core/skills/`, `core/operating/` and `core/templates/`
  are not vendored — grepped: not one `judgement-only` row's mandate quote appears anywhere in a built
  arm, and `AGENTS.md` never names `clarify`, `codify` or `consolidate`. The artifact does not say so.
- **`compile` advertises a flag it does not have.** [`../../cli/compile.mjs`](../../cli/compile.mjs)
  tells the operator to *"pass `--runner`/`--stop-runner` to name a path you control"*; measured,
  `compile: unknown argument "--runner"`. The options exist programmatically only. **A remedy an error
  message names and the tool refuses** — found because I tested a supervisor's claim instead of building
  on it, and it redirects 6b: ship the two flags rather than re-implement compile's write path, which
  would be a second implementation of what `compile` emits.

## Where the measurements disagreed with a ruling, and with me

**Ruling 8 was retired by ruling 12.** *"Constitution copied ephemerally, byte-verified"* was taken while
arm A was to be customer zero's workspace entire; retargeting removed its premise. `docs/vision.md` is
**Portulan's** product constitution, so copying it in makes arm A resemble customer zero — which the
discriminator refuses. Recorded as retired by the maintainer rather than left for the specification to
win quietly.

**And one of my own measurements was wrong for an hour.** I read `compile --help`'s *"emits RESTRICTION
only"*, concluded the compile half was inert for this corpus, then designed scenarios whose verdicts are
**side-effects** and did not re-derive the conclusion against the new design. Compiled enforcement is
decisive here. The retraction is in the plan's record rather than deleted from it.

**Round 3's finding arrived in the SUPPRESSED channel, and it was a stale count in this file.** The

**Round 4 caught a false claim about the mechanism, and correcting it exposed a wrong correction of my
own.** `rule-carriers.json` said its tells were LINE-LEVEL fragments, "which is the granularity the
scanner actually reads". It is not: `normalise()` collapses every whitespace run and strips emphasis,
code markers and link markup, so the real constraint is **contiguity after normalisation** — a tell may
span a prose wrap freely, and cannot span a `# ` comment prefix or a string-literal boundary, because
those survive.

_The sequence matters more than the sentence._ The pre-commit checkpoint measured **two** of four
spellings dead. I re-measured with `git grep -F`, got **three**, and reported the checkpoint as having

confident false correction of a correct verdict, and it went into a handoff and a message to the
maintainer before Copilot's suppressed channel caught the claim it rested on. Measured through
`normalise` itself: two alive, two dead, exactly as the checkpoint said. **Re-measuring a supervisor's
figure is right; re-measuring it with a different instrument and not noticing is how that goes wrong.**

**Round 2's body was 127 bytes**, which is close enough to the *"unable to review"* shape this
repository has met before (117 bytes) that it was **read rather than counted**: it says *"reviewed 10
out of 10 changed files and generated no new comments"*, which is an empty round and not an error. The
review's `commit_id` was checked against `HEAD` and `origin`, because an empty round closes nothing if
anything was pushed after it.
